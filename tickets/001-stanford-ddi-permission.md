# 001 · Ask Stanford for permission to use the Diverse Dermatology Images

**Status:** open, not ready to send
**Opened:** 2026-10-06

## Why

Darker skin is the biggest gap in Worth a Look: 64 of 9,565 photos (see the "Known gap" section of the project page). Stanford's [Diverse Dermatology Images](https://ddi-dataset.github.io/) (DDI) has 656 biopsy-confirmed photos across all skin tones, built to compare light and dark skin. Its research use agreement forbids publishing or reproducing the images "without written permission", so the only route is to ask.

The DDI paper itself makes the case: dermatologists, not just AI models, did worse on darker skin ([Daneshjou et al., Science Advances, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35960806/)). Worth a Look trains the people who see skin every day.

## What would make the request credible

A cold email from an individual with a hobby project is easy to decline. These would change that, roughly in order of impact:

1. **A dermatologist or skin cancer researcher as co-signer.** Someone from a group that already trains hair and skin professionals is the best fit, for example the Oregon Health & Science University team behind the skin-aware professionals course ([Diehl et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39219284/)) or IMPACT Melanoma. The same person could review `public/notes.js`, which is also an open item.
2. **Evidence it gets used.** Feedback from the barber pilot, and before-and-after skill check scores from a few testers (shared by them voluntarily; the game collects nothing).
3. **A narrow, specific ask.** Display only, inside a free, non-commercial, open-source teaching game, with each photo credited, no download or redistribution of the files, and removal on request.
4. **An ask that helps everyone, not just this project.** The best outcome is the DDI team releasing some or all photos under an open license, for example by contributing them to the ISIC Archive. Offer that as the first option and permission for this project as the fallback.

## Where to send

The DDI page lists the dataset on the [Stanford AIMI shared datasets portal](https://stanfordaimi.azurewebsites.net/datasets/35866158-8196-48d8-87bf-50dca81df965). Find the current contact there, or write to the paper's corresponding authors.
