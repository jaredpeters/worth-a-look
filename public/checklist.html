Is this spot worth a look?

Suggest a doctor if any one of these is true.

If none of them fits but something still bothers you or your client, suggest a doctor anyway. This list helps you notice. It can't tell you a spot is safe.

1New, different or changing

  • A new spot, or a spot that looks different from their other moles: the odd one out.123
  • Changing in size, shape or color.14
  • Raised, firm and growing over weeks. It can be one even color, including pink or red, and often doesn't show the A to D signs below.29

2How it looks

  • Asymmetry: one half doesn't match the other.1
  • Border: ragged, scalloped or blurry edge.1
  • Color: more than one color, such as tan, brown and black, or patches of white, red or blue.1
  • Diameter: wider than about 6 mm, the size of a pencil eraser. Melanomas can be smaller, so a small spot isn't cleared by size.1

3Sores, bumps and rough patches

  • A spot or patch that hurts, itches, bleeds, crusts or scabs for more than 4 weeks.5
  • A sore that doesn't heal, or heals and comes back.67
  • A shiny bump: pink or red on lighter skin, brown or black on darker skin.6
  • A rough, scaly patch, or a growth like a wart or a small horn.7

4On darker skin, also check

  • A brown or black streak under a nail. It's easy to mistake for a bruise.8
  • A growing dark spot on a palm or sole, especially with more than one color or an uneven edge.8
  • A darker patch on a lip or inside the mouth.8

What to say

"I noticed a spot on your [scalp / neck / face / ear]. It might be worth having a doctor look at it."

Say what you saw and where. Don't guess what it is.
