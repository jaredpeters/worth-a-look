Swipe through real skin spots. Learn which ones need a doctor.

Skinder shows you photos of moles and skin growths that were later checked by biopsy. You swipe left if it looks fine and right if you'd get it checked, and the real answer appears straight away. After a few hundred swipes you start to see what doctors look for.

How it works

1

Look

Each card is one real spot, with the person's age, sex and where it was on the body. Doctors use those too.

2

Swipe

Left means it looks fine. Right means you'd get it checked. Buttons and arrow keys work as well.

3

Learn

The biopsy result appears with the diagnosis. Any photo you got wrong comes back once, about 30 cards later.

Where the photos come from

Every photo comes from the ISIC Archive, a public collection of skin images run by the International Skin Imaging Collaboration. Skinder keeps only photos whose diagnosis was confirmed by biopsy: a doctor removed the spot and a lab examined it under a microscope. Photos the lab couldn't call either way are left out, because there would be no answer to score you against.

Photos load straight from the archive into your browser. Each answer card shows the photo's license and who took it, with a link to its page in the archive.

Naked eye

6,159 ordinary close-up photos, like one you'd take with a phone. This is the view you have of your own skin.

4,781 cancers and 1,378 harmless spots. Each round is dealt half and half.

Dermoscope

3,000 photos through a dermatoscope, the lit magnifier a skin doctor presses against the skin. Patterns show up that you can't see by eye.

1,500 cancers and 1,500 harmless spots.

What it can't do

  1. It can't tell you about your own skin. It's a practice game. Anything new, changing, bleeding or worrying you should be seen by a doctor, whatever your score says.
  2. Half of every round is cancer. In real life almost every spot is harmless. The even split is there so you get enough practice on both, but it can make every mole start to look suspicious.
  3. The harmless photos are harder than real life. They were suspicious enough that a doctor took a biopsy. Your everyday moles look far more ordinary.
  4. Most naked-eye cancers are basal cell carcinoma, the most common and least dangerous skin cancer. Melanoma practice is stronger in dermoscope mode.
  5. Most photos show lighter skin. Skin cancer can look different on darker skin. Adding a dataset like Stanford's Diverse Dermatology Images is on the list.

Run it yourself

It's a static web page with a small Node server. No build step, no database.

git clone https://github.com/jaredpeters/skinder.git
cd skinder
npm install
npm start            # open http://localhost:8140

Or with Docker: docker compose up -d.

The list of photos ships with the repo. To fetch a fresh copy from the archive, run python3 scripts/build_deck.py. It takes a few minutes and uses only the Python standard library.

Skinder is a practice game. It doesn't diagnose anything. If a spot on your skin worries you, see a doctor.
